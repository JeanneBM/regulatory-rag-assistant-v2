# Manual review of the 2026-11-13 pilot

Run: `eval-20261113T115349Z` (full mode, local TF-IDF retrieval). Reviewed by Claude (Opus 5.5) on 2026-11-13 against the recorded quotations only. This is not legal advice; the partial and wrong rows should be confirmed by a person. Labels per claim are in [eval-20261113T115349Z-review.json](eval-20261113T115349Z-review.json).

Question for every claim: is the statement fully supported by its quotation, including conditions and exceptions?

## Result

| | Supported | Partial | Wrong |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Shown to the user (21) | 15 | 4 | 2 |
| Rejected by verification (5) | 1 | 2 | 2 |

- **The model verifier labelled all 21 shown claims `supported` and never used `partial`.** Two shown claims change the legal meaning: AI Act Article 66, point (f) turns "the Board may" into "shall", and the Article 5(1)(c) claim invents an exception for research.
- **All five rejections came from the figure rule reading excerpt IDs as numbers.** The model wrote "(E2, E3)" or "according to E3" into statements; "E3" counted as the figure 3, which the quotation lacks. This removed two wrong claims and three that were correct or nearly so, all for the wrong reason, and none of them reached the model verifier. The same artefact let the invented exception through: its "(E1)" matched the "1." of the quotation.
- **D02 produced no answer.** The model returned five claims, the limit is four, and the whole answer was discarded. Its retrieval was not recorded, so the retrieval figures (7/8) count a generation error as a retrieval miss.
- **Per-claim checks cannot see omissions across provisions.** In A04 every claim matches its quotation, but the answer leaves out Article 53(2), which exempts providers of qualifying open-source models from points (a) and (b).

## What changed after this review

Replayed on these 26 claims, the new deterministic rules (`tests/test_pilot_review.py`) flag 0 of the 16 claims reviewed as supported, and flag 5 of the 10 that are not: Article 4 (dropped qualifier), Article 66(f) (power presented as an obligation), Article 5(1)(c) (invented exception), Article 6(3) (dropped Annex III scope) and Article 50(3) (dropped exception). The other five, including the wrong Article 2(8) claim, now go to the model verifier, which answers five checks before labelling. That verifier change has not been run against a model yet.

These rules were written after seeing these claims, so the replay shows they do what was intended here, not how they generalise. The next pilot should add questions written without looking at these results.
